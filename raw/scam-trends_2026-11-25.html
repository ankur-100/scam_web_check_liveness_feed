Advisory: Beware of Fake Singapore Police Force Websites and Messages

drbypass-proxy.pages.dev

The Singapore Police Force (SPF) has observed a trend of scam websites and messages impersonating official SPF communications. These fraudulent sites aim to trick the public into believing they are interacting with the police, often to solicit personal information or money.

How to recognise these scams:

  • Unusual website addresses: Official SPF websites always end with .gov.sg. Be wary of links that use different endings (e.g., .pages.dev, .com, .net) or contain suspicious characters.
  • Requests for sensitive information: SPF will never ask for your banking details, Singpass password, or one-time passwords (OTPs) through unofficial channels or suspicious links.

What to do if you encounter such a scam or have been affected: If you receive a suspicious message or encounter a website claiming to be SPF, do not click on any links, provide personal details, or make any payments. If you have already done so, immediately stop all communication with the scammers, secure your bank accounts and digital profiles, and make a police report. Inform your family and friends about the scam to help them stay vigilant.

Stay Vigilant Against Job Scams Impersonating TikTok

globaltop.org

The Singapore Police Force has observed a recent trend of job scams impersonating well-known brands like TikTok. Scammers are using fake websites and messaging platforms to trick individuals into believing they are applying for legitimate employment opportunities.

Here's how to recognise these scams:

  • Unsolicited Job Offers: You receive unexpected job offers, often via messaging apps or social media, claiming to be from a well-known company like TikTok, promising easy money for simple tasks.
  • Suspicious Links and Websites: These offers often direct you to dubious websites, such as globaltop.org, or require you to communicate via unofficial channels. They may ask for personal details or upfront payments for "training" or "registration."

If you encounter such job offers or suspect you have fallen victim to this scam:

  • Tell the authorities. Report the incident to the Police immediately. If you have shared your banking details or transferred money, inform your bank to take necessary precautions. Share this information with your family and friends to help them stay safe.

Beware of Ninjavan Phishing Scams: Verify Delivery Links

oouyif.cfd
nrgfzf.cfd
nlqqgz.cfd
oclaro.cfd
ltftri.cfd
nsnmtq.cfd
oonpyg.cfd

nlraro.cfd

The Singapore Police Force has observed a trend of scam websites impersonating parcel delivery service Ninjavan. These scams often trick victims into clicking malicious links in unexpected messages, leading them to fake websites that solicit personal and banking details under the guise of delivery issues or unpaid fees.

How to recognise this scam:

  • Suspicious Website Addresses: Scammers use fake websites with unusual domain names (e.g., ending with .cfd) that do not belong to Ninjavan's official website (ninjavan.co).
  • Unexpected Messages: You receive SMS or emails claiming there are issues with your parcel delivery, a missed delivery, or that you need to pay a small fee to reschedule, often prompting you to click a link.

What to do if you were affected:

If you suspect you have fallen victim to this scam, take these immediate steps:

  • If you provided banking details, contact your bank immediately to cancel your cards and monitor your accounts for any unauthorised transactions.
  • Change passwords for any online accounts where you might have used similar credentials or shared details on the fake site.
  • Report the incident to the police.

I can ACT against scams:

  • Add security features: Enhance your online security by enabling Two-Factor Authentication (2FA) for all your online accounts, especially banking and e-commerce platforms.
  • Check for scam signs: Always verify any suspicious links or messages directly with Ninjavan through their official app, website, or customer service hotline. Do not click on suspicious links.
  • Tell authorities, family and friends: If you encounter such a scam, report it to the police and inform your family and friends to prevent them from falling victim.

security-platform.vip

The Singapore Police Force has observed a trend of fake investment scams impersonating well-known brands like NVIDIA, often through suspicious websites such as 'security-platform.vip'. These scams aim to trick you into investing in fraudulent schemes.

How to recognise this scam:

  • Scammers create fake websites or platforms that look legitimate, using brand logos and names like NVIDIA to promote fraudulent investment schemes.

What to do if you encounter or are affected by this scam:

  • If you have fallen victim:
    • Add security features: Change passwords for all your online accounts, especially banking and email, if you've shared any details. Enable two-factor authentication (2FA) wherever possible. Contact your bank immediately to freeze your accounts if money has been transferred.
    • Tell authorities, family and friends: Report the incident to the police and inform your loved ones about this scam so they can be vigilant too.

Protect Your Singpass Account from Phishing Scams

fuxxaway.workers.dev

The Singapore Police Force has observed a trend of phishing scam sites impersonating Singpass. These scam sites aim to trick individuals into revealing their Singpass login credentials.

How to recognise these scams:

  • Always check the website address (URL) carefully. Official Singpass websites will always end with singpass.gov.sg. Scammers often use similar-looking but fake website addresses, such as fuxxaway.workers.dev.
  • Be wary of unexpected messages, emails, or links asking you to click and log in to your Singpass account. Official Singpass communications will not typically ask you to log in via a link.

What to do if you encounter or are affected by such scams:

  • Check: If you suspect a website is fake, do not proceed with logging in. Close the browser tab immediately.
  • Tell: If you have accidentally entered your Singpass details on a suspicious website, change your Singpass password immediately. You can do this through the official Singpass app or website.
  • Tell: Report the incident to the Police.
  • Tell: Alert your family and friends about these scams so they can protect themselves too.

akk500.com

The Singapore Police Force has observed a trend of investment scams impersonating well-known brands, including 'Koka Kona'. Scammers are creating fake websites and online platforms to trick individuals into bogus investment schemes. We urge the public to remain vigilant against such fraudulent activities.

Here's how to recognise such scams:

  • Scammers create fake websites or applications (e.g., akk500.com) that closely mimic legitimate brands like 'Koka Kona' to promote fraudulent investment opportunities.

If you encounter such a scam or have already been affected, remember to ACT:

  • Add security features: Secure your online accounts with strong, unique passwords and enable two-factor authentication (2FA) wherever possible. This helps prevent unauthorised access.
  • Check for scam signs: Always verify any investment offers by contacting the official company or financial institution directly through their official channels (e.g., official website, known customer service number), not through links or numbers provided in suspicious messages. Never click on unverified links or download suspicious apps.
  • Tell authorities, family and friends: If you suspect you've fallen victim, immediately cease all contact with the scammer, report the incident to your bank, and then file a police report. Share your experience with family and friends to protect them from similar scams.

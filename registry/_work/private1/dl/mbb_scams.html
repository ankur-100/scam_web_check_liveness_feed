Advisory on Phishing Scams Impersonating Courier Companies

23 November 2026

Beware of phishing scams involving scammers impersonating courier companies through unsolicited messages. Victims may receive messages claiming that a parcel delivery has failed due to an incorrect or incomplete address and are urged to update their delivery details within a short period of time.

The messages may appear to come from familiar or legitimate courier companies and contain links resembling their official websites. In some cases, scammers may also use genuine company information or customer support details to make the messages appear more convincing.

Victims who click on these links may be directed to a spoofed website and asked to pay a small ‘re-delivery fee’, followed by requests for their banking or card details and One-Time Passwords (OTPs). These details may then be used to make unauthorised transactions.

 

How can you protect yourself from falling prey to scams?

  • Be wary of unsolicited delivery messages that create urgency such as requests to update your address or make payment within a short period of time.
  • Do not click on links or reply to suspicious messages even if they appear to come from a familiar courier company. Verify the request directly through the courier's official website or app.
  • Never disclose your banking or card credentials, including passwords, One-Time Passwords (OTPs) or card CVV numbers, or make payments through unverified links.
  • Look out for unusual senders and website addresses including overseas numbers, random email addresses or links that closely resemble, but are not, the courier's official website.
  • Report suspicious or unauthorised transactions immediately by calling 1800-MAYBANK (1800-629 2265) or (65) 6533 5229 (Overseas) and press *1. Lodge a police report immediately if you suspect that you have fallen victim to a scam.

Advisory on Phishing Scams involving Hotels and Hotel Booking Platforms

28 October 2026

Beware of phishing scams involving hotels and hotel booking platforms where scammers impersonate hotels or booking platforms through WhatsApp messages or emails. Victims may be asked to verify their card details or confirm an upcoming accommodation booking. These messages may even contain legitimate booking details, making them appear genuine.

Victims may then be directed to a phishing website to "secure" or confirm their booking and prompted to provide their card details or authorise a transaction, sometimes with claims that no payment will be charged. Victims may only realise they have been scammed after noticing unauthorised charges or upon verifying directly with the hotel.

 

How can you protect yourself from falling prey to scams?

  • Always verify booking-related requests directly with the hotel or official booking platform, even if the message contains legitimate booking information.
  • Do not click on links in unsolicited messages or emails, especially those asking you to verify or confirm your booking or payment details.
  • Never disclose your banking or card credentials, including passwords, One-Time Passwords (OTPs) or card CVV numbers. Do not authorise transactions you did not initiate.
  • Call 1800-MAYBANK (1800-629 2265) or (65) 6533 5229 (Overseas) and press STAR *1 to report any suspicious or unauthorised transactions made to your account(s) and lodge a police report.

Stay Alert to Inheritance Scam Letters

5 October 2026

Maybank has received reports of scam letters falsely claiming that recipients are entitled to inherit funds or assets from a deceased individual. Maybank does not request upfront payments, banking credentials or One-Time Passwords (OTPs) in connection with inheritance or estate-related matters.

Fraudsters may impersonate lawyers, wealth managers or financial institutions and claim that you have been identified as a beneficiary, often because you share the same surname as the purported deceased. Recipients are typically promised substantial inheritances and asked to provide personal information, banking details or make upfront payments to facilitate the release of funds.

 

Warning Signs

  • Unsolicited inheritance or estate-related letters.
  • Promises of substantial inheritances or unclaimed assets.
  • Requests for personal, identity or banking information.
  • Demands for taxes, processing fees or other upfront payments.
  • Pressure to act urgently or keep the matter confidential.

 

Protect Yourself

  • Verify any inheritance or estate claim through official and independent channels.
  • Do not share your banking credentials, card details or OTPs.
  • Do not make any payment to claim an inheritance.
  • Cease communication immediately if you suspect a scam.
  • Report suspicious or unauthorised transactions immediately by calling 1800-MAYBANK (1800-629 2265) or (65) 6533 5229 (Overseas) and press *1. Lodge a police report immediately if you suspect that you have fallen victim to a scam.

Victims may be falsely accused of money laundering or other criminal activities and instructed to meet at a purported "Maybank Back Office" or locations near Maybank branches as part of a supposed investigation.   • Never share your Singpass ID, banking credentials, One-Time Passwords (OTPs), card details (including CVV) or other sensitive information with anyone.
  • Report suspicious or unauthorised transactions immediately by calling 1800-MAYBANK (1800-629 2265) or (65) 6533 5229 (Overseas) and press *1. Lodge a police report immediately if you suspect that you have fallen victim to a scam.

Advisory on Investment Scams Involving Chat Groups Offering “Free” Investment Learning

10 June 2026

Beware of scammers using fake social media advertisements offering opportunities to learn investing for free. Victims may be invited to join messaging platforms (e.g. WhatsApp) where scammers pose as “mentors” and share purported investment advice or bonuses to build trust.

Within these chat groups, other “members” may post screenshots of supposed trading profits to create a false impression of success and encourage victims to start investing. Victims may then be instructed to open accounts on fraudulent investment platforms or transfer money to designated bank accounts, and in some cases, hand over cash or valuables to individuals posing as company representatives.

To further deceive victims, scammers may provide initial “returns” to create a false sense of legitimacy. The scam is typically uncovered only when victims are unable to withdraw their “profits” or when the scammers become uncontactable.

 

How can you protect yourself from falling prey to scams?

  • Do not accept unsolicited invitations to investment chat groups or offers promising free learning or guaranteed returns.
  • Do not transfer money or hand over cash, valuables or personal details to individuals whose identities you cannot verify.
  • Be cautious of unfamiliar investment platforms or applications and check their authenticity and user reviews before use.
  • Verify the legitimacy of investment opportunities and companies through official sources by confirming the company’s credentials and representatives’ credentials using available resources including the Financial Institutions Directory, Register of Representatives and Investor Alert List on the MAS’ website.
  • Lodge a police report as soon as you suspect that you have fallen victim to a scam.

Advisory on E-Commerce Scams involving the Sale of Concert Tickets

9 June 2026

Beware of scammers offering concert tickets such as those for the BTS World Tour ‘Arirang’ concert. They may use screenshots or videos of fake tickets or receipts to appear legitimate and create urgency by claiming tickets are limited or time-sensitive.

Victims are typically instructed to make payment via PayNow or by scanning a PayNow QR code. After the initial payment, scammers may request additional payments under various pretexts (e.g. administrative fees or unsuccessful transactions). Scammers may promise ticket delivery after payment but later claim that the initial payment was not received and continue to solicit further payments.

Victims usually realise they have been scammed only when the tickets are not delivered or when the seller becomes uncontactable.

 

How can you protect yourself from falling prey to scams?

  • Do not scan QR codes from unsolicited messages sent via SMS, WhatsApp, email or social media platforms.
  • Purchase tickets only from authorised platforms such as Ticketmaster and do not trust tickets sent via email as Ticketmaster will never issue concert tickets through email.
  • Be cautious of resellers claiming they can transfer tickets to your Ticketmaster account as tickets are strictly not transferable or for resale.
  • Exercise caution when viewing ticket listings on social marketplace platforms — Facebook Marketplace does not allow content promoting the buying, selling or trading of event tickets and TikTok Shop’s policies do not support the sale of concert tickets.
  • Lodge a police report as soon as you suspect that you have fallen victim to a scam.

Advisory on Impersonation Scams arising from Compromised WhatsApp Accounts

12 May 2026

Beware of scammers attempting to take over WhatsApp accounts by entering victims' mobile numbers and triggering a WhatsApp verification one-time password (OTP) request sent to the victims. Scammers may then impersonate a known contact and claim the OTP was sent by mistake to trick victims into disclosing it.

Once the WhatsApp account is compromised, scammers impersonate the victim to contact their family or friends, citing urgent pretexts such as emergency loans and pressuring them to transfer money via digital payment methods including PayNow QR codes to digital payment service providers such as Razer Merchant Services and Grab.

Victims typically realise they have been scammed only after verifying the requests directly with the actual WhatsApp accounts' users.

 

How can you protect yourself from falling prey to scam?

  • Be cautious of unusual or urgent money requests received over WhatsApp and never share your WhatsApp OTP with anyone.
  • Secure your account by removing unknown linked devices linked to your WhatsApp account via "Settings > Linked Devices".
  • Enable the 'Two-Step Verification' feature on your WhatsApp account via "Settings > Account > Two-step verification > Enable".
  • Never disclose your Singpass ID, banking or card credentials such as username, password, One-Time Password (OTP) or Card CVV numbers to others.
  • Call 1800-MAYBANK (1800-629 2265) or (65) 6533 5229 (Overseas) and press STAR *1 to report any unauthorised transactions made to your account(s) and lodge a police report.

Government Official Impersonation Scams

16 March 2026

Beware of scammers impersonating government officials and instructing victims to hand over valuables such as gold bars, jewellery or luxury watches.

Victims may receive unsolicited calls from scammers posing as representatives from insurance, financial institutions or retailers, alleging outstanding insurance premiums or unauthorised card transactions. When victims deny these claims, they may be transferred to another scammer impersonating officials from agencies such as the Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS) or the Ministry of Law (MinLaw).

Victims are then accused of involvement in criminal activities (e.g. money laundering) and may be provided with fake documents such as a "warrant of arrest" or a "financial inspection order". They may be instructed to hand over valuables to unknown individuals for purported "investigation purposes" including items newly purchased under the scammers' instructions or valuables owned by the victims.

Victims usually realise they have been scammed only when the scammers become uncontactable or after verifying the matter with the relevant authorities.

 

How can you protect yourself from scams?

  • Never transfer or hand over money, valuables, cryptocurrency or luxury items to unknown persons.
  • Always verify the authenticity of the information with the official websites or sources.
  • Never disclose your Singpass ID, banking or card credentials such as username, password, One-Time Password (OTP) or Card CVV numbers to others.
  • Call 1800-MAYBANK (1800-629 2265) or (65) 6533 5229 (Overseas) and press STAR *1 to report any unauthorised transactions made to your account(s) and lodge a police report.

Advisory on Investment Scams involving Shell Investment Applications that Promote Fraudulent Investment Products

16 December 2025

Beware of scammers promoting fake investment products through shell investment applications. Victims are often enticed by attractive social media advertisements that falsely promise guaranteed high returns on investments.

Victims were first contacted via WhatsApp and subsequently invited to join group chats filled with scammers posing as successful investors. In these chat groups, victims were persuaded to download seemingly genuine trading applications from mobile app stores, not realising these were in fact fake platforms displaying fabricated trades and profits.

Victims were then instructed to transfer money to specified bank accounts or via a QR code to YouTrip to start the "investment". In some cases, victims were asked to meet unknown individuals in person to hand over cash or gold to "invest" in these fraudulent investment products. Victims were issued fake documents as proof of their investment deposits and only realised that they had been scammed when they were unable to withdraw their money.

 

How to protect yourself from investment scams?

  • Activate your device’s privacy function to prevent unknown persons from adding you to their group chats.
  • Always check for user reviews and ratings of application before downloading them, even if they are believed to be legitimate and are available on official application stores.
  • Verify the authenticity of the investment company by confirming the company’s credentials and representatives’ credentials using available resources including the Financial Institutions Directory, Register of Representatives, and Investor Alert List on the MAS’ website.
  • Call 1800-MAYBANK (1800-629 2265) or (65) 6533 5229 (Overseas) and press STAR *1 to report any unauthorised transactions made to your account(s) and lodge a police report.

Advisory on SMS Stealer Targeting Android Device Users

8 August 2024

The Cyber Security Agency of Singapore (CSA) alerted to a mobile malware that can scan SMSes to retrieve one-time passwords (OTP). The SMS stealer is distributed via malicious advertisements or Telegram bots that can automatically communicate with victims.

The malicious app, disguised as an Android application package (APK) is then generated for tracking and potential future cyber attacks targeting the victim.

  • Install only apps from the official Google Play Store. Check the developer information on the app listing, and download only apps developed and listed by the official developer.
  • Avoid disabling the Play Protect function that runs safety checks on apps from the Google Play Store before downloading them.
  • Read the security permissions requested by the app and its privacy policy before downloading. Be wary of apps that ask for unnecessary permissions like accessing the SMS function or contact list on a device.
  • Immediately uninstall any unknown apps that suddenly appear on devices.
  • Perform anti-virus scans and keep regular backups of important data.
  • Ensure that devices’ operating systems and apps are updated regularly so that they are protected by the latest security patches.
  • Never disclose your Singpass ID, banking or card credentials such as username, password, One-Time Password (OTP) or Card CVV numbers to others.

Re-emergence of bulk order scams

Beware of the re-emergence of bulk order scams targeting businesses in the renovation, food and beverage, retail and service industries.

Advisory on Protecting Yourself From Malicious QR Codes

2 May 2024

Beware of the dangers and potential threats of malicious Quick Response (QR) codes as they could trick unsuspecting individuals into scanning these codes and consequently exposing themselves to various threats. Some of the common types of attacks involving QR codes are as follows:

  • Phishing- Malicious QR codes are used to redirect unsuspecting individuals to phishing websites masquerading as legitimate ones to steal sensitive information such as login credentials, credit card numbers or other personal data.
  • QR Code Swaps- Legitimate QR codes displayed as businesses are altered or tampered with in order to trick individuals into redirecting payments to a scammer’s account.
  • Malware Infection- Scammers may use QR codes to trick you into downloading malware from an app store or website.  They seek permission to download other apps, read your SMSes or look through your phone.  Saying ‘yes’ to a permission request will give scammers the opportunity to control your device, spy on your chats, steal your sensitive information and steal your money through funds transfers or credit card charges. 

 

How can you protect yourself from Malicious QR Codes?

  • Avoid scanning QR codes received via unsolicited text, WhatsApp messages, E-mails or social media messaging platforms.
  • Look out for signs that the QR codes at public places have been tampered with. Only download files or applications from official and verified sources.
  • Verify the recipients of the digital payments through QR codes before confirming payment.
  • Never disclose your Singpass ID, banking or card credentials such as username, password, One-Time Password (OTP) or Card CVV numbers to others.
  • Call 1800-MAYBANK (1800-629 2265) or (65) 6533 5229 (Overseas) and press STAR *1 to report any unauthorised transactions made to your account(s) and lodge a police report.

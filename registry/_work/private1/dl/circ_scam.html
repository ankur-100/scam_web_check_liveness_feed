Scam calls are more than just an annoying interruption—they’re a major threat to your personal and financial security. In recent years, these fraudulent schemes have surged, costing victims billions globally. In Singapore alone, scams resulted in over SGD $660 million in losses in 2022.

If you’ve ever received a suspicious call offering a "prize," threatening legal action, or asking for sensitive information, you’ve likely been targeted by scammers. The good news? You can fight back. Learning how to report scam calls can protect you and help prevent others from falling victim. Filing a phone fraud report and learning how to report numbers as scammers or report number scam strengthens efforts to fight phone fraud.

This guide will show you why reporting scam calls is essential, how to take action, and how to safeguard yourself from fraud.

Reasons to Report Scam Calls

Protect Yourself and Others

When you report scam calls, you protect not just yourself but also others in your community. Reporting allows law enforcement and telecom providers to take action, such as blocking fraudulent numbers and raising public awareness. Every time you report a number as a scammer’s, you’re contributing to a safer communication environment.

Support Authorities in Taking Action

Filing a telephone fraud reporting provides authorities with the details they need to track scammers and shut down their operations. Information like phone numbers, call times, and suspicious requests are critical for investigations. Your effort to report number scams makes a real difference in holding scammers accountable.

Prevent Financial and Data Loss

Scammers are often after personal data or financial information. By acting quickly to report fraud phone calls, you can reduce the risk of financial loss or identity theft for yourself and others.

[H2]Identifying and Reporting Scam Calls

Identifying a Scam Call

The first step in protecting yourself is recognising a scam call. Here are some common warning signs:

  • Unsolicited Offers or Prizes: The caller claims you’ve won a lottery, prize, or exclusive offer—especially if you didn’t participate.

  • Requests for Sensitive Information: Scammers often ask for your NRIC, bank account details, or OTPs. Legitimate organisations will never request this information over the phone.

  • Impersonation of Authorities or Brands: Scammers frequently pretend to be government officials, banks, or telecom providers to gain your trust.

  • Urgency or Threats: Scammers use fear tactics, claiming that immediate action is needed to avoid fines, legal trouble, or arrests.

If you’re unsure about a call, hang up and verify the information independently using official contact numbers or websites.

Steps to Report Scam Call

Gather Information

Before you report phone scams, collect the following details:

  • The phone number used by the scammer.

  • The date and time of the call.

  • A brief summary of the conversation, including any threats or suspicious requests.

This information will make your phone fraud report as detailed and helpful as possible.

Report to Singapore Scam Hotline

Most countries have a scam hotline where you can report fraudulent calls. In Singapore, call the Anti-Scam Hotline (1800-722-6688) to report the scam and provide details about the incident. If you're searching for more information, look for resources using phrases like “report number scam Singapore” or “report number as scammer Singapore.”

File a Report Online

In Singapore, filing a report online is a straightforward process. Visit the ScamShield website to report scam calls. The platform is managed by the Singapore Police Force and the National Crime Prevention Council (NCPC), allowing you to provide detailed information about the incident.

When filing your report, include:

  • The phone number used by the scammer.

  • The date and time of the call.

  • Details of the conversation, such as the type of scam, any threats made, or suspicious requests.

Additionally, you can upload any supporting evidence, such as screenshots of messages or call logs. Submitting a detailed report helps authorities track down scammers and warn others in the community.

For further guidance, you can call the Anti-Scam Hotline at 1800-722-6688. They can assist you in understanding the reporting process and provide advice on staying safe from scams in the future.

Remember, your action to report scams online strengthens the collective effort to combat fraud in Singapore.

Notify Your Telecom Provider

Your telecom provider, such as Circles.Life, plays an important role in combating scam calls. Notify your provider to block suspicious numbers and assist in investigating the scam. When you report a number as a scammer to your telecom carrier, you help improve security for everyone in their network.

Use Reporting Apps

Apps like Truecaller, Hiya, and RoboKiller let you flag and block scam numbers. While these apps are helpful in identifying and managing scam calls, they are not official reporting channels. To ensure that your report is acted upon by the authorities, it is essential to report scam numbers to official platforms such as the ScamShield website or the Anti-Scam Hotline (1800-722-6688). Contributing to these official channels strengthens the collective effort to combat scams effectively.

Tips to Protect Yourself from Scam Calls

Use Call Blocking Apps

Apps like Truecaller, RoboKiller, and Hiya are great tools for identifying and blocking scam calls. These apps let you flag and report number scams to build a stronger global defense against fraud.

Avoid Sharing Personal Information

Never disclose sensitive information like your NRIC, bank account details, passwords, or OTPs over the phone. Scammers often pose as legitimate entities to trick you, but remember: real organisations will never ask for this information through unsolicited calls.

Verify the Caller’s Identity

If someone claims to represent a trusted organisation, hang up and contact the official hotline listed on the company’s website. This simple step can save you from falling victim to fraud.

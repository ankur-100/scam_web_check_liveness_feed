Advisory on phishing scams and good cybersecurity practices

The protection of our customers’ personal data is of utmost importance to us. Singapore Airlines (SIA) advises customers to remain vigilant against scams and adopt good cybersecurity practices.

Common Type of Scams

Scammers may:

  • Post fake contact details on the Internet, Google Maps, wiki pages, and community forums.
  • Impersonate SIA through phone calls, social media accounts, messaging apps, or fraudulent email addresses. They may request for personal information, flight details, login credentials, or banking and credit card details. SIA will never ask customers to disclose their password or one-time password (OTP) to us by phone, email, or SMS. Do not disclose these to anyone, including someone claiming to be a SIA employee. Only enter your OTP on our official website or mobile app.
  • Offer fraudulent job opportunities. All available job vacancies  are advertised on our official Careers portal and LinkedIn.
  • Publish fraudulent advertisements, requests, or giveaways to collect personal data or bank card details, or to entice people to click on phishing links or open malicious email attachments.

More information about scams may be found on the ScamShield website.

 

How SIA Helps Protect Customers from Scams

Our customers’ online safety and security are important to us. To protect our customers from fraudulent text messages, SIA has registered our sender IDs with Singapore’s SMS Sender ID Registry.

Messages sent to Singapore mobile numbers (+65) from unregistered sender IDs will be labelled “Likely-SCAM”. However, customers should remain vigilant, even if a message does not carry this label.

When customers report a potential scam, SIA will check if their booking or KrisFlyer account have been affected. Where necessary, scam sites and social media accounts that reflect contact details or impersonate SIA are reported to the relevant authorities and parties for removal.

 

Good Cybersecurity Practices

To safeguard against scams and phishing attempts:

1.      Always verify before you act

  • Access your booking or KrisFlyer account only through our official website or the SingaporeAir mobile app.
  • Avoid clicking on links, scanning QR codes, opening attachments, or installing software at the request of unknown or suspicious senders.
  • Do not use contact details provided by unverified sources. Customers who require assistance are advised to contact SIA using the details listed on our official website.
  • To verify whether a call, text message, email, or social media account is genuine, or to report a suspected fake SIA website, please share the details via this link.

 

2.      Protect your bookings and accounts

  • Keep your registered contact details up to date.
  • Use a strong, unique password for your KrisFlyer account and a different password for your email account.
  • Always login to your account using our official website or mobile app. Download the SingaporeAir mobile app from an official app store.
  • Enable two-factor authentication (2FA) for flight bookings made through the SIA website or SingaporeAir mobile app. This provides an additional layer of security by requiring a time-sensitive, randomly generated OTP before you can access customer details or change or cancel a booking. Find out more about 2FA here.  

More information on keeping your personal data safe may be found here.

 

What to Do If You Suspect a Scam

If you think you may have fallen victim to a scam, act immediately.

  • Shared your banking or card details, or made a payment? Contact your bank or card issuer immediately.
  • Disclosed your account password? Change it immediately through our official website or mobile app.
  • Shared an OTP or noticed suspicious booking or KrisFlyer activity? Contact SIA immediately through our official contact channels.

Keep screenshots, messages, and transaction records as evidence, and lodge a police report. You may also call the 24/7 ScamShield Helpline at 1799 for assistance.

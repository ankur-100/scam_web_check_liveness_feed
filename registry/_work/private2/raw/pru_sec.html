    Stay Vigilant Against Malicious Attachments Sent Through Messaging and Social Media Platforms

    Date: 16 September, 2026


    We would like to alert you to an increase in cyber threats involving malicious attachments distributed through messaging and social media platforms such as WhatsApp, Facebook Messenger, Telegram, Instagram, and other chat applications. Cybercriminals may compromise legitimate accounts and use them to send messages that appear to come from trusted contacts. These messages often contain attachments disguised as invoices, payment confirmations, account statements, or other financial documents.

    To protect yourself:

    • Be cautious when receiving unexpected attachments or files, even if they appear to come from someone you know.
    • Verify the authenticity of any financial or payment-related documents through a separate trusted communication channel before opening them.
    • Do not download or open attachments from unsolicited or suspicious messages.
    • Be cautious of messages that create urgency or request immediate action.
    • Do not share personal, banking, or policy-related information through unverified channels.
    • Keep your devices and security software updated with the latest security patches.

     

    If you have opened a suspicious attachment or interacted with a suspicious message:

    • Stop all further interaction with the suspicious content immediately.
    • Close any suspicious files, applications, or websites.
    • Run a full security scan using updated anti-virus or anti-malware software.
    • Change your passwords if you suspect any personal information or credentials may have been compromised.
    • Monitor your accounts for any unusual activity.
    • Contact us at 1800-333-0-333 if you require assistance or have concerns relating to your Prudential policies or accounts.

     

    Some Key Reminders
     

    Messaging Platforms

    Prudential corporate office does not use WhatsApp or other messaging platforms for official customer communications. If you are communicating with your Financial Representative through WhatsApp or other messaging applications, always verify their identity before sharing information or acting on instructions.

     

    Unexpected Attachments

    Be especially cautious of unexpected invoices, payment records, account statements, or other financial documents received through messaging or social media platforms, even when they appear to originate from a trusted contact.

    Stay Vigilant: Callers Impersonating Prudential staff or Prudential Financial Representatives

    Date: June 22, 2026

     

    [Updated 23 June 2026] We wish to alert you to a recent surge in scam calls from individuals impersonating Prudential staff or financial representatives. These calls have been received by both our customers and members of the public, with the callers speaking in English and Mandarin. Please be vigilant should you receive such calls as they are fraudulent.
     

    Types of Calls Reported

    • Claims of Outstanding Premiums:
      Callers inform recipients that there is an existing Prudential policy with unpaid premiums that require immediate action.

    • Misuse of Personal Information:
      When recipients indicate they do not hold such a policy, callers may claim that their personal details have been used by someone else to purchase a policy and offer to “assist” in resolving the issue.

    • False Cash Payouts:
      In some cases, callers mention a payout or cash benefit and ask recipients to click on a link to verify or process the payment.

    • Fake Policy Documents:
      Callers may say a new policy has been created and offer to send a PDF, followed by links for the recipient to review or act.

    • Redirection via Links:
      Across these calls, recipients are directed to click on links to verify details or proceed further. These links are not legitimate.

     

    Protect Yourself

    To help you stay protected, please follow these important precautions:

    • Do Not Click on Any Links:
      Do not click on any links sent via SMS, messaging apps, or shared during such calls.

    • Do Not Engage with the Caller:
      Do not continue the conversation or follow any instructions given.

    • Do Not Share Your Information:
      Never provide your personal, banking or policy details over unsolicited calls.

    • Verify Directly with Prudential:
      If in doubt, contact us through our official channels to confirm.

     

    If you have interacted with such a caller or clicked on any links:

    • Stop immediately. Close the website and do not download any files or applications from it. (If possible, take a screenshot first as evidence.)
    • Clear your browser history and cache to remove any traces of the suspicious site.
    • Contact your bank immediately if you have shared any financial or banking details.
    • Monitor your accounts closely for any unusual activity.
    • Change your passwords without delay if any personal information has been exposed, including for your:
      • Email accounts
      • Banking applications
      • Any other accounts using the same password
         
    • Run a security scan on your device using updated anti-virus or anti-malware software.
    • Reach out to us at 1800-333-0-333, so we can support you further.
    • You may also report scams via ScamShield (1799) or visit www.scamshield.gov.sg.

    Stay vigilant: Recent scams involving fake staff members from another Insurer

    Date: March 9, 2025

     

    We wish to alert you to a recent surge in scams involving impersonation of insurance companies and regulatory authorities. These scams have resulted in significant financial losses for many individuals. We urge you to read the following information carefully to protect yourself from falling victim to these fraudulent activities.

    Common Scam Techniques

    1. Impersonation of Officials:
      • Scammers pose as representatives from a local insurance company and the Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS).
      • They may contact you via phone calls, video calls, emails, or messaging apps, claiming you have outstanding insurance premiums or policies that need urgent attention.
         
    2. Urgent Payment Requests:
      • You may be told that you need to pay fees to renew or cancel an insurance policy.
      • Scammers often create a sense of urgency, insisting that immediate action is required to avoid automatic deductions of the fee from your bank account.
         
    3. Remote Access and Information Harvesting:
      • Scammers may ask you to share personal and banking information or to perform actions such as logging into your bank account while they guide you through the process.
      • They might use screen-sharing functions or video calls to appear more legitimate.
         
    4. False Claims of Fraudulent Activity:
      • You may be informed that your bank account is linked to illegal activities or that your personal information has been compromised.
      • Scammers will then instruct you to transfer money to a specified account to "verify" or "secure" your funds.
         

    PROTECT YOURSELF.

    To help you stay protected, please follow these important precautions:

    1. Do Not Share Your Banking Details: Never share any of your banking details like UserID or Passwords with a third party, either over the phone or over a video. Prudential and our representatives will never ask you for these details.
    2. Payment Links: Prudential will never send you a link to make a payment. Customers should visit our website and click on Online Payment or make payment via AXS or internet banking.
    3. Cash Collection: Our Financial Representatives will also never ask you to transfer any money to them or pay them cash. If you encounter this, please report it to us immediately.
    4. Phone Numbers and Website Addresses: Be cautious of the phone numbers and website addresses you interact with. Always ensure they are legitimate.
    5. Stay Safe When Downloading Apps: Never download applications from links provided by unknown or unverified sources. Always install Prudential and banking-related applications from official app stores such as the Google Play Store or Apple App Store, or through links available on Prudential’s official website. If you are unsure about an application's authenticity, please verify it before installation.

    Insurance payout scam

    Date: 16 August 2023

     

    We have been notified of a recent scam attempt where an individual (“A”) borrowed money from someone (the victim) by misleading the victim into believing that A is the beneficiary of several insurance policies and will be repaying the loan using the payout from these policies.

    In this case, A met a female individual on a dating app, who later claimed to be bankrupt and in need of money. She requested for a loan and as an assurance that she will be able to repay A, she misled A to believe she is the beneficiary of two Prudential insurance policies using forged documents which stated she will be receiving payouts from the policies soon. The victim transferred several sums of money to the female individual.

    Soon after, A contacted Prudential to verify the validity of the insurance policy document he had received, and upon verification by our service staff, it was found that the policy documents he received were forged. He proceeded to lodge a police report immediately.

    We would like to remind our customers to remain vigilant at all times: Protect your banking and insurance accounts by not sharing your log-in details with others, and not performing financial transactions on anyone else’s behalf. You should also not transfer money to others until you have you been able to verify the legitimacy of their requests.

    Should you encounter any suspicious activities, please do not hesitate to contact your Financial Consultant or call us at PRUCustomer Line at 1800-333-0-333.

    Helpful information

    WHAT ARE LOAN SCAMS?

    SMSes or WhatsApp messages that are sent by scammers disguised as licensed banks / financial providers offering loan services to random users. As part of these scams, scammers may ask for users personal information like Singpass details, NRIC, bank account numbers and mobile numbers. Interested parties are then told to transfer money as a deposit before the loan can be disbursed to them. After making the transfer, users find that the scammers are no longer contactable and end up being victims to these loan scams.

    Avoid responding to or contacting the numbers in such SMSes.
